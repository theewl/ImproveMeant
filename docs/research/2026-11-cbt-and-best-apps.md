## Part A — Clinical evidence

### A1. NICE CG159 (Social anxiety disorder: recognition, assessment and treatment, 2013; last reviewed 2024)
- **First line for adults:** "Offer adults with social anxiety disorder individual cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) that has been specifically developed to treat social anxiety disorder (based on the Clark and Wells model or the Heimberg model)." [S] https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/CG159/chapter/Key-priorities-for-implementation — accessed 2026-11-02
- **If CBT is declined:** "For adults who decline individual CBT and wish to consider another psychological intervention, offer CBT-based supported self-help." [S] same URL; also summarised at https://www.learnsignal.com/blog/social-anxiety-disorder-nice-cg159-care-staff-guide/ — accessed 2026-11-02
- Group CBT should not be offered routinely in preference to individual CBT. [S] https://nice.org.uk/guidance/cg159/evidence/surveillance-review-decision-september-2015-pdf-547753069 — accessed 2026-11-02
- Individual CBT is up to 14 sessions of about 90 min. Supported self-help is about 3–4 hours of practitioner time with a CBT workbook. Mindfulness and supportive therapy are "not routinely" offered. [K] **I could not confirm these from snippets.** Check recs 1.3.x at https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg159/chapter/Recommendations
- **Network meta-analysis behind CG159:** Mayo-Wilson et al., *Lancet Psychiatry* 2014 (101 trials). Individual CBT had the largest effect among psychological treatments, and "self-help with support" also beat waitlist. [S]/[K] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4287862 — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Implication for Clearline:** the *guideline-endorsed* role for self-help is as **supported** self-help, an alternative to therapy, not a replacement for clinician-led CT-SAD. Clearline should present itself as skills practice and signpost to professional care.

### A2. Clark & Wells cognitive model / CT-SAD components
- **The model.** Clark & Wells (1995), "A cognitive model of social phobia," in Heimberg et al. (eds.), *Social Phobia* (Guilford). Its maintaining factors are negative social beliefs, self-focused attention with a negative self-image, safety behaviours, and pre- and post-event processing. Most of its hypotheses have empirical support. [S] https://www.psychologytools.com/tools/pdf-tools/cognitive-behavioral-model-of-social-phobia-clark-wells-1995 ; https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/27591/ — accessed 2026-11-02
- **The protocol.** A 14-session CT protocol with large effect sizes across RCTs (Clark et al. 2003, 2006; Stangier et al. 2003). Clark et al. 2006 (*JCCP* 74:568–578) beat exposure plus applied relaxation. [S] https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/27591/ ; https://www.proquest.com/docview/213089396 — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Self-focused attention.** In social situations the person shifts attention inward and builds a felt-sense self-image ("I look visibly shaking"). In one study, moving to external focus mediated later symptom improvement. [S] https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/publications/2084886 — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Safety behaviours.** These are subtle avoidance moves, such as rehearsing sentences, avoiding eye contact, or gripping notes. They prevent disconfirmation and can make performance look worse. They are tested in the "self-focused attention & safety behaviours experiment." [S] https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/publications/2084886 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5091738 — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Behavioural experiments.** The person writes a specific prediction ("people will notice I'm shaking; 80% sure"), runs the situation (often without safety behaviours), then compares the outcome. The SFA/SB experiment significantly reduced social anxiety in the following month of treatment (Schreiber 2015, n=32). [S] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5091738 — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Video feedback.** The person predicts how they will look, then watches the recording as an observer would. In an adolescent iCT-SAD sample (n=35), video review produced more positive appraisals of appearance and performance. Small and preliminary. [S] https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/98509 — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Anticipatory and post-event processing.** Anticipatory processing is worry before the event. Post-event processing is the "post-mortem" afterwards, which replays the negative self-image and keeps beliefs alive. Both are named model variables. [S] https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/27591/ — accessed 2026-11-02. The detail on post-event processing is [K]; I found no dedicated PEP review in this session.

### A3. Internet / app-based / self-guided CBT
- **Kampmann, Emmelkamp & Morina 2016** (*J Anxiety Disord* 42:71–84; 37 RCTs, N≈2,991) found "substantial evidence for ICBT" and preliminary evidence for VRET. Cognitive bias modification showed only small effects. Unguided ICBT vs passive control: **g = 0.78** (95% CI 0.50–1.05, k=5). [S] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27376634/ ; https://nationalelfservice.net/treatment/other/internet-based-treatment/digital-interventions-for-social-anxiety-disorder-new-meta-analysis-finds-mixed-results — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Guided vs unguided (anxiety broadly).** A meta-analysis of 11 head-to-head studies (n=1,414) found a small post-treatment advantage for guided ICBT (**g = 0.16**, 95% CI 0.03–0.28) and no significant difference at follow-up (g = 0.13). [S] https://core.ac.uk/download/30790951.pdf — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Social anxiety RCTs.** Titov et al. ("Shyness 3", n=98): clinician-assisted vs self-guided vs waitlist, with a between-group ES of about 1.04 for clinician-assisted vs control. Guided beat self-guided in that trial. [S] https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/shyness-3-randomized-controlled-trial-of-guided-versus-unguided-i/ — Berger et al. 2011 (*BRAT*) found **no difference** between unguided, guided and tailored self-help on dropout, adherence or remission (d=1.47 within-group). [S] https://boris.unibe.ch/43662 — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Limitations (honest):** (1) Trial participants are self-selected, screened and often monitored, so open-access apps see far higher dropout. (2) Many comparisons are against waitlist, which inflates effects. (3) Few RCTs exist of *smartphone apps* (rather than web programmes) for SAD; I found no 2023–26 app-only meta-analysis in this session. (4) Within-group d values are not comparable to between-group g values. (5) Human support tends to improve adherence even when the outcome gap is small. [S]/[K] — sources as above
- **Implication:** Content should be structured like a programme (psychoeducation, then experiments and exposure, then relapse-prevention), not a loose toolbox. Lightweight human-style support, such as check-ins and progress feedback, likely aids adherence.

### A4. Exposure hierarchies, SUDS, and simulated audiences
- **Fear ladder.** List feared situations, rate each one, and order them least to most feared. Exposures should be "planned, prolonged and repeated," and the ladder should cover the full range. Anxiety Canada's handout uses a **0–10** fear scale. [S] https://admin.heretohelp.bc.ca/sites/default/files/facing-your-fears-exposure.pdf — accessed 2026-11-02
- **SUDS** (Subjective Units of Distress) runs 0 (none) to 100 (worst ever). The hierarchy should span the full SUDS range so the top fear is eventually faced. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposure_hierarchy — accessed 2026-11-02 (secondary)
- **Inhibitory learning (Craske et al. 2014, *BRAT* 58:10–23).** The goal is **expectancy violation**, not just habituation. Ask "what do you expect will happen?" before, and "what actually happened?" after. Other strategies: remove safety signals, vary the exposure, use multiple contexts, use affect labelling. [S] https://contextualscience.org/publications/craske_treanor_conway_zbozinek_vervliet_2014 ; https://escholarship.org/content/qt5x26t4nd/qt5x26t4nd.pdf?t=rsp58k — accessed 2026-11-02. **This supports a prediction-vs-outcome rating design over a pure SUDS-drop design.**
- **VR / simulated audience.** A meta-analysis of public-speaking anxiety (Reeves et al. 2022, *Behavior Modification*) found VRET reduced PSA vs control (SMD −1.39, p<.001). A 2023 RCT meta-analysis of VRET for SAD found large effects vs waitlist but a small advantage for in-vivo exposure over VRET, and few RCTs overall. [S] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10106288/ ; https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-the-efficacy-of-virtual-reality-and-in-vivo-ex/ — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Implication:** Clearline's simulated audience is a plausible *low rung* of the ladder. Real-world exposures should be the higher rungs. Don't imply that VR or simulation alone is equivalent to therapy.

### A5. Cognitive restructuring — "thinking traps" in free public resources
- **Anxiety Canada / HeretoHelp (BC):** catastrophizing, fortune-telling, mind-reading, over-estimating danger, emotional reasoning, and more. The poster and worksheets are free to read. [S] https://admin.heretohelp.bc.ca/sites/default/files/thinking-traps-poster.pdf ; https://bc.cmha.ca/covid-19-take-care-of-yourself/challenge-thinking-traps/ — accessed 2026-11-02
- **NHS:** "Unhelpful thinking styles" leaflets (e.g. RNOH) include mind-reading, defined as "assuming we know what someone else is thinking… and that they are negative". [S] https://www.rnoh.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/patient-information-guides/unhelpful-thinking-styles — accessed 2026-11-02
- **CCI (Western Australia):** the original "Shy No Longer" modules (2003) have been replaced by **"Stepping Out of Social Anxiety"** consumer modules, which are free to read online and include a module on challenging unhelpful thinking. [S] https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/News/New-Consumer-Modules-for-Social-Anxiety-Now-Online ; https://cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/For-Clinicians/Social-Anxiety — accessed 2026-11-02

The concepts (CBT thinking-trap categories, fear ladders) are generic and in wide use, so **we can write our own wording and examples inspired by them.** We must not copy text, worksheets or layouts, and we may cite them as "further reading."
- **Note:** Anxiety Canada's **MindShift CBT app was discontinued** (no updates or support after 31 Mar 2025; user data deleted). The organisation reported funding difficulties in 2025, so its web resources may not stay online. [S] https://apps.apple.com/app/mindshift-cbt-anxiety-relief/id634684825 ; https://ground.news/article/he-felt-chased-by-anxiety-and-got-help-online-now-that-resource-experts-trust-could-be-shut-down — accessed 2026-11-02

### A6. Safety practices and the regulatory line
- **Crisis resources:** US **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline**: call or text 988, chat at 988lifeline.org/chat, veterans press 1, TTY via 711. It is free, 24/7, and needs no personal information. [S] https://www.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/988-factsheet.pdf — accessed 2026-11-02. International: **findahelpline.com** (Throughline directory) [K, not search-verified]. UK: NHS 111, Samaritans 116 123 [K]. Show these in a persistent "Get help now" item, not only in onboarding.
- **When to recommend professional help:** when distress or avoidance seriously impairs work, study or relationships; when there is low mood, hopelessness or self-harm thoughts; or when there is no improvement after several weeks of practice. NICE positions individual CBT as first line. [S] NICE KPI URL above; thresholds are [K] product judgement. A brief, non-diagnostic self-check such as a Mini-SPIN-style screen could trigger a signpost, but must not label anyone with a diagnosis [K].
- **FDA:** "General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices" was **revised 6 Jan 2026** (superseding 2019). Low-risk products intended *solely* for general wellness, such as stress management and confidence, fall under enforcement discretion. Claims to diagnose or treat a disease or condition (e.g. "treats social anxiety disorder") move a product toward device regulation. Mental health and vulnerable users get heightened scrutiny. [S] https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download ; https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12657.html ; https://www.faegredrinker.com/zh/insights/publications/2026/1/key-updates-in-fdas-2026-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-software-guidance — accessed 2026-11-02
- **UK MHRA:** "Digital mental health technology: qualification and classification" guidance (Feb 2025). **Intended purpose**, meaning what the maker claims, decides whether a product is a medical device. General-wellbeing tools may not qualify, while tools to diagnose or treat a condition do. [S] https://www.gov.uk/government/news/digital-mental-health-technologies-guidance-launched-to-help-manufacturers-and-safeguard-users ; https://www.hilldickinson.com/our-view/articles/regulatory-updates-on-digital-mental-health-technologies/ — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Cautionary precedents:** Woebot retired its consumer app on 30 Jun 2025, and its founder cited the cost of meeting FDA marketing-authorisation requirements [S] https://www.statnews.com/2025/07/02/woebot-mental-health-chatbot-shuts-down-founder-says-ai-moving-faster-than-regulators/. Lumosity paid $2M to settle FTC charges over unsubstantiated "brain training" claims (2016) [S] https://www.ftc.gov/node/44800 — accessed 2026-11-02

### A7. Retrieval practice and spacing for adult vocabulary
- **Cepeda et al. 2006** (*Psych Bull*): a distributed-practice meta-analysis (verbal recall) found spaced practice beats massed, and the optimal gap grows with the retention interval. [S] https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/CPVWR2006.html — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Kim & Webb 2022** (*Language Learning* 72(1)): a meta-analysis of spaced practice in L2 learning (vocabulary-heavy) supports spacing. Moderators include number of sessions, practice type, feedback timing and retention interval. [S] https://ir.lib.uwo.ca/etd/8600/ ; https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/ssllt/article/download/35602/30649/76142 — accessed 2026-11-02
- **Bahrick et al. 1993:** 9-year foreign-vocabulary retention benefited from longer spacing. [S] https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/1993-bahrick.pdf — accessed 2026-11-02. Karpicke & Roediger 2008 (*Science*) found that retrieval, not re-study, drove retention of foreign vocabulary [K].

Avoid dependence on a single grant funder, and give users data export so their data isn't lost. [S] App Store URL above.

Borrow: conversational check-ins and a short skill picker. Avoid implying regulatory approval. [S] https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220512005084/en — accessed 2026-11-02

[S] https://blogs.perficient.com/2023/12/22/sanvello-a-holistic-approach-to-psychological-and-psychiatric-well-being ; https://www.choosingtherapy.com/?p=1460 — accessed 2026-11-02

### Memory / vocab / habit
- **Anki + FSRS:** FSRS is built in since 23.10, with a user-set **desired retention** (0.70–0.99; 0.90 is a sensible default) and fewer reviews than SM-2 for the same retention. Borrow: an FSRS scheduler and an exposed retention target. Avoid Anki's review-pile overwhelm by capping daily new items. ## Top 10 design takeaways for Clearline
1. **Prediction → outcome loop on every exposure.** Before: "What do you predict will happen? How sure are you (0–100)? Expected anxiety (0–10)?" After: "What happened? Actual peak anxiety? What did you learn?" Show the gap over time. This puts expectancy violation and behavioural experiments into the product (A2, A4).
2. **Graded exposure ladder** built with the user. Simulated audience (small and friendly, then larger and distracting, then hostile Q&A) as low rungs, real-world tasks as high rungs. Use a 0–10 scale in the UI and store 0–100 internally if needed (A4).
3. **Safety-behaviour drop experiments.** Ask users to tick their usual safety behaviours, then run paired practice *with* and *without* one, rating anxiety and self-rated performance (A2).
4. **Attention-out cue.** During the simulated-audience run, show a prompt to look at faces and listen to the audience. **No real-time filler-word or "um" counters in anxiety mode**, since they feed self-focused attention. Feedback should come afterwards (A2, Poised).
5. **Video/audio feedback done right.** Predict how you'll come across, *then* review the recording "as an observer," then re-rate. Pair this with neutral metrics after the review (A2).
6. **Post-event-processing guard.** After practice, a 60-second "close the loop" (one thing that went OK, one thing to try next) followed by an explicit "done, no replay" ending. Anticipation check-ins should be brief and time-boxed (A2).
7. **Thinking-trap check in our own words**, with mind-reading, fortune-telling, catastrophizing, spotlight effect and discounting positives as the core five for speaking. Use an evidence-for/against card and a balanced thought. Original content only; link free resources as further reading (A5).
8. **Programme arc, not a toolbox:** roughly 6–8 short modules (understand the cycle, then attention, then safety behaviours, then experiments and ladder, then thoughts, then relapse plan), with weekly progress review à la Sanvello and Toastmasters-style levels (A3, B).
9. **Gentle habit design:** 3–5 minute daily check-ins, reminders at a time the user picks, progress shown as "practice count / ladder rungs climbed / predictions disconfirmed." **No loss-framed streaks or guilt notifications.** If streaks exist, make them forgiving and opt-in (B: Duolingo).
10. **Spaced review for ideas and vocabulary** using FSRS with roughly 90% desired retention, active recall (say it aloud before revealing), and capped daily new cards. Reuse the same review engine for "key insights from my experiments" (A7, Anki).

Also: a persistent **"Get help now"** item (988 / findahelpline / local), a non-diagnostic self-check that signposts to professional care, and data export.

## Claims we must not make
- Clearline does not "treat," "cure," "diagnose," or "manage" social anxiety disorder or any other condition, and is not "therapy," "CBT treatment," or "a substitute for a therapist." Acceptable language is "CBT-informed self-help skills" and "practice tools for speaking confidence."
- Do not claim to be "clinically proven," "evidence-based treatment," "FDA-approved/cleared," "NHS-recommended," or "endorsed by NICE." NICE recommends *supported self-help* generally; it does not recommend Clearline.
- Do not borrow outcome statistics from trials of other programmes ("ICBT has g = 0.78, so Clearline works").
- No uncontrolled outcome marketing ("90% of users feel less anxious") without competent and reliable evidence (FTC/Lumosity precedent).
- Do not present the simulated audience as "VR exposure therapy" or say it is equivalent to working with a clinician.
- Do not imply the AI coach is a counsellor or that it monitors for crisis. State that it cannot respond to emergencies and point to 988 or local services.
- Do not reuse Anxiety Canada, CCI or NHS text or worksheets, and do not imply affiliation with them.
